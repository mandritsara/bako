Malagasy–Tsimihety Grammar Lesson 16A

The Passive Voice

Malagasy–Tsimihety with Bako

1. The importance of the passive voice

In the Malagasy language, the person and the object are just as important as the action, or whoever does the action. The passive voice is a way of building a sentence to show this. In Malagasy, the passive voice is common, even as natural as the active voice. What we pay attention to and what we understand depend on the voice used in the sentence, even if the end result is the same.

The passive voice is used to put the emphasis on the object (a person or a thing) rather than on the action, or on the subject / actor as in the active voice. So if you want to emphasise the person or the thing, you need to use the passive voice, which answers the questions IZA no … ? (off) / ZOVY … ? (tsm) (Who is … ?) or INONA no … ? (off) / INO … ? (tsm) (What is … ?)

Let us take the example 'wash the clothes':

WASH   manasa (active) – sasana (passive)

THE CLOTHES   ny lamba

In an active-voice sentence, 'manasa lamba', the emphasis is on washing (a Malagasy speaker hears what is being done), while in a passive-voice sentence, 'sasana ny lamba', the emphasis is on the clothes (a Malagasy speaker hears what is being washed). Yet in the end, the clothes are washed in both cases.

– Active voice – emphasis on the action

Manasa ovy izy (He washes potatoes)

• Manasa ovy izy (fa tsy mitetika ovy).
He washes potatoes (he does not chop potatoes).

– Passive voice – emphasis on the object

Sasany ny ovy (He washes the potatoes)

• Sasany ny ovy (fa tsy ny karaoty).
He washes the potatoes (not the carrots).

Active voice – emphasis on the action or the subject

Ex:

• Manasa ovy izy (fa tsy mitetika karaoty).
He washes potatoes (he does not chop carrots).

• Izy no manasa ovy (fa tsy I Soa.)
It is he who washes the potatoes (not Soa).
