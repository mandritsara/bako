Theme 11A — Asking for Directions

Manontany sy manoro làlana/na toerana

Asking for and giving directions to a road/place

1. Vocabulary for "road" and related terms

Làlana (off) / làlagna (tsm) = road

This is the Malagasy word for road, but there are other expressions:

  • Arabe = street (usually in town and paved)
  • Hitsin-dàlana / làlamanitsy = shortcut
  • Làlantany = dirt track / unpaved road
  • Làlan-tsarety = poor road, only passable by carts
  • Làlankely = alley / small path
  • Làlamby = railway
  • Làlan'ny bisikileta = bicycle lane

2. Talking about distance

  • Distance is relative: what some people call "far," others call "close," depending on their habits of travel.
  • In rural areas, kilometres are not commonly used to describe distance. Instead, people speak in terms of time using the word ADINY (off) / LERA (tsm) = hour.
Làlana adiny 3 (off) = làlagna 3 lera (tsm) → "a 3-hour journey" (by foot or vehicle).

3. Using cardinal points for directions

To indicate a location, a place, or a region, Malagasy people often use the cardinal points.

AvaratraNorthMianavaratra (off) / Magnavaratra (tsm)Go north
AtsimoSouthMianatsimo (off) / Magnatsimo (tsm)Go south
Andrefana (off) / andrefagna (tsm)WestMiankandrefana (off) / Magnandrefagna (tsm)Go west
Atsinanana (off) / atinianagna (tsm)EastMiatsinanana (off) / Magninianagna (tsm)Go east

4. Question words for place and direction

There are three common question words used for places and directions:

AIZA? = Where?

  • Aiza ny hopitaly, azafady? → "Please, where is the hospital?"
  • Aiza ny làlana, azafady? → "Please, where is the road to … ?"

MANDEHA AIZA? / MANKAIZA? = Go where? / To where?

  • Mankaiza ity làlana ity, azafady? → "Please, where does this road lead?"

AVY AIZA? = From where?

  • Avy aiza ity fiarandalamby ity? → "Where is this train coming from?"

5. Common expressions with làlana

Questions: Manahoana ny làlana? (off) / Manakory ny làlagna? (tsm) = How is the road?

Answer: ……………… ny làlana.

  • Ratsy ny làlana = The road is bad
  • Tsara ny làlana = The road is good

6. Example Dialogue: "Where do we meet?"

Aiza ny fotoanantsika?

a: Aiza ny fotoanantsika?

(Where is our meeting place?)

b: Any an-tsena.

(At the market.)

a: Aiza ho aiza any an-tsena?

(Where exactly in the market?)

b: Ampitan'ny mpivaro-damba.

(Across from the fabric seller.)

a: Aiza ny mpivaro-damba lazainao?

(Where is the fabric seller you mean?)

b: Hitanao ve ny mpivaro-trondro?

(Do you see the fishmonger?)

a: Eny, akaikin'ny mpivarotra lasantsy.

(Yes, near the fuel seller.)

b: Manaraka ny mpivaro-trondro, misy mpivarotra legioma. Atsinanan'ny mpivarotra legioma, misy mpivaro-boankazo.

(Next to the fishmonger is the greengrocer. East of the greengrocer is the fruit seller.)

a: Hitako, misy mpivaro-mofo andamosin'ny mpivarotra legioma sy voankazo.

(I see, there's a bakery behind the fruit and vegetable sellers.)

b: Eny, dia akaikin'ny mpivaro-mofo ny mpivaro-damba.

(Yes, and near the bakery is the fabric seller.)

a: Andrasako ampitan'ny mpivaro-damba ianao.

(I'll wait across from the fabric seller.)

b: Eny, eo anoloan'ny fivarotam-panafody. Mety ve amin'ny 9?

(Yes, in front of the pharmacy. Is 9 o'clock OK?)

a: Eny, mety amin'ny 9.

(Yes, 9 o'clock is fine.)

7. Vocabulary

Nouns:

Tsena (off) / bazary (tsm)Market → any an-tsena = at the market
MpivarotraSeller / merchant
Mpivaro-dambaFabric seller / fabric shop
Mpivaro-trondroFishmonger / fish seller
Mpivarotra lasantsyFuel seller / fuel station
Mpivarotra legiomaGreengrocer (vegetables)
Mpivaro-boankazoFruit seller / greengrocer
Mpivaro-mofoBaker / bakery
FivarotanaShop / store
Fivarotam-panafodyPharmacy
Ny fotoanaThe meeting → ny fotoanantsika = our meeting
AtsinananaEast

Note: both mpivarotra and fivarotana are used — Mpivarotra fanafody = seller of medicine; Fivarotam-panafody = pharmacy.

Verbs:

LazainaoYou say / the one you are talking about
HitanaoYou see / do you know?
HitakoI see / I know
MisyThere is / there are
AndrasakoI wait for … (Andrasako ianao = I'm waiting for you)
MetyRight / OK / fine (Mety ve? = is it OK?)

Prepositions of place:

AmpitaAcross
AkaikyNear, next to
Manaraka nyNext to, following
AndamosinaBehind
AnoloanaIn front of

Other:

  • Eny = yes
  • Dia = then
  • Amin'ny 9 = at 9 o'clock
