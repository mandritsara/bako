Theme 13 — Ny Raharaha ao an-trano

Chores

Background

Many Malagasy families still live from farming, livestock, fishing, and crafts. The tasks of each family member can vary according to different factors — geographical, economic, social, and family circumstances.

Women more often stay at home, looking after the household and children, at least when the children are young, or they work from home to earn money. Traditionally, Malagasy women are responsible for managing the home and finances, the family's wellbeing, organising daily life, and the care and education of the children. Some also do gardening, sewing, or weaving in addition.

Providing for and protecting the family, and making important decisions, are the responsibilities of the men — the head of the family. Although women are responsible for household tasks, men can help and participate in them.

In general, there are more typically feminine tasks such as preparing meals, cleaning the house, doing the laundry, fetching water, looking after young children, and tending the farmyard animals. More typically masculine tasks include building and repairing the house, looking after the zebus, collecting firewood, and working in the fields.

When children are young, they are taught traditional values, social rules, and the skills needed to succeed in life — they are already being prepared for their future roles. You can see this in play: boys make omby tanimanga (zebus made of clay); girls play tsikonona (cooking, but in small quantities). Children participate in household chores according to their age, and sometimes, unfortunately, children have to help their parents by working.
